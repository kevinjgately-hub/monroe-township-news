Board of Education · Written-Records Roundup

Putnam Valley's school board this year: a $65.3 million budget, a new trustee and a fall of school reports

Voters passed the 2026–27 budget 985 to 876 in May and elected Jay Lee to the board. This fall the board has heard opening-of-school reports from all three schools, a look at the senior citizen tax exemption and new achievement data. Its next meeting is December 10.

Nov 25, 2026 · Compiled from the school district's website: budget documents, election results, meeting schedule and posted presentations, not from recordings · 4 min read

Quick facts

What
The Putnam Valley Central School District's Board of Education, which runs the town's elementary, middle and high schools
When it meets
Usually twice a month at 7 p.m. in the high school cafeteria, 146 Peekskill Hollow Road; meetings are open to the public and streamed live
Leadership
Superintendent Dr. Jeremy Luft; Barbara Parmly was board president as of the spring
How to read this
The board's agendas and minutes are on BoardDocs, which we have not yet reviewed, and its meetings are on video. This roundup covers what the district has posted on its own website.

1. Voters pass a $65.3 million budget, and Jay Lee wins a seat

On May 19, voters approved the district's 2026–27 budget of $65,254,332 by 985 to 876, according to the district's posted results. The budget is up $1,922,590, or 3.04%, from the year before. It raises $43,412,658 from property taxes, up 1.98%, which the district says keeps it within the state tax cap for the 15th year in a row. If the budget had failed, the district would have had to adopt a "contingency" budget of $64,409,969. A separate ballot question asked voters to approve $514,500 for the Putnam Valley Free Library.

Two candidates ran for one three-year seat on the board. Jay Lee won with 900 votes to Crystal Hernandez's 836; there were 29 write-in votes.

2. A summer of construction

Reporting to the Town Board on July 15, the district's representative said the summer projects included rebuilding the high school and middle school parking lots and replacing the last section of the high school roof, which is being prepared for future solar panels. The middle school, high school and athletic fields were closed to the public over the summer. The district also listed a special board meeting on August 10 at 8 a.m. to declare an emergency for repairs to fireproofing material in the high school.

3. What the board has heard this fall

The district has posted video of its August 25, September 10, September 24, October 8 and October 20 meetings, and slides from five fall presentations:

  • September 24: the elementary school's opening-of-school report.
  • October 8: the middle school's opening-of-school report.
  • October 20: the high school's opening-of-school report, and an analysis of the district's senior citizen property tax exemption, the partial school-tax break New York lets districts give older homeowners with limited incomes.
  • November 17: a presentation of student achievement data. Before that meeting the superintendent held a "listening hour" for residents in the high school library.

We have not yet reviewed these recordings and presentations in detail, so we are not reporting what they said or what the board decided. We plan to recap upcoming meetings.

Next meetings

The board's next business meeting is Thursday, December 10, at 7 p.m. After that come a work session and business meeting on January 7, 2027 and a business meeting on January 21. Dates can change; check the district's Board of Education page.

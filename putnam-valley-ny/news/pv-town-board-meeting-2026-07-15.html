Town Board · Meeting Recap

Everything in the minutes of Putnam Valley's July 15 Town Board meeting

The board buys two electric-vehicle chargers for Town Hall with a $29,822 grant, pays $332,873 toward the Cimarron Road bridge, fixes a salary typo in the union contract, and hears about senior scams, summer school construction and a lost trail grant.

Jul 15, 2026 · Compiled from the official minutes posted on the town website, not from the recording · 5 min read

Quick facts

What
Regular meeting of the Putnam Valley Town Board
When
Wednesday, July 15, 2026, 6 p.m.; adjourned 7:33 p.m.
Where
Town Hall, 265 Oscawana Lake Road
Who
Supervisor Alison Jolicoeur and Council Members Louie Luongo, Christian Russo, Sherry Howard and CJ Brooks, all present
Votes
Every resolution passed unanimously

1. Two EV chargers for Town Hall

The board approved buying two dual-port ChargePoint Level 2 electric-vehicle charging stations for the Town Hall campus, near the parking area, to be installed along with parking lot improvements. (A Level 2 charger is the common 240-volt type that adds roughly a full charge over several hours.) A grant of about $29,822 covers most of the cost, and the town pays a 20% match of about $7,455. The minutes say the chargers were first considered for the Tompkins Corners property but were moved because of redevelopment plans there, and that the public and town employees will pay a fee to use them, which offsets costs. During public comment a resident welcomed the chargers, noting there is little public charging nearby. (A week earlier, at its July 8 work session, the board authorized going out to bid for the installation work.)

2. Another payment on the Cimarron Road bridge

The board added an item to the agenda and approved a second installment of $332,873.21 to Kingston Equipment Rental for the Cimarron Road bridge project, signed off by Town Engineer Todd Atkinson. Highway Superintendent Shawn Keeler reported the project was on schedule and asked drivers to slow down on Horton Hollow during the detour. (The bridge has since reopened; see our fall roundup.)

3. Union contract fixes and personnel

  • The board ratified a supplemental agreement adding a Senior Real Property Appraiser title to the town's contract with CSEA, the union for many town employees, for 2026 through 2029, and provisionally reclassified an employee in the Assessor's office into the new title, pending a Civil Service exam.
  • It corrected an error in the contract's 2027 salary schedule: the Principal Clerk I, Step 3 salary should be $63,790, not $69,791.
  • It hired a temporary laborer for the Lake Peekskill Improvement District at $16 an hour, as needed, to cover an employee's medical leave, with the district paying.
  • The board met behind closed doors from 6:58 to 7:19 p.m. to discuss personnel, took no action, and then removed two items, a code enforcement officer and an assistant building inspector, from the agenda.

4. Bills and refunds

The board approved three groups of bills totaling $645,465.27 ($476,302.79, $59,521.94 and $109,640.54) and nine refunds totaling $3,723, mostly $500 deposits and camp and program refunds.

5. Reports

  • Scams: Putnam County District Attorney Bob Tendy warned about scams aimed at seniors, including fake grandchildren, impersonators, package-delivery and home-repair schemes, romance and tech-support scams, malicious QR codes and AI voice cloning. His advice: don't share personal or financial information by phone, verify requests independently and limit vacation posts on social media.
  • Highway: Milling and repaving had begun, but some planned roads were dropped because of rising prices. Crews had repaired 78 storm drain basins and cleaned about 475 in Lake Peekskill. Keeler explained the state's MS4 stormwater program, which requires towns to document drain maintenance, and said the department is moving to electronic records on two new field tablets. He asked residents to call the highway department, which answers 24/7, rather than post complaints on Facebook.
  • Parks: The town did not win a trail grant and plans a smaller trail improvement in-house. A forest logging project was on schedule for bidding and permitting and headed to the Planning Board. The bike path was to be repaved and painted, Independence Day drew about 1,500 people, and Thursday concerts ran through August 20.
  • Schools: The school district's representative said the high school and middle school parking lots were being rebuilt and the last section of the high school roof replaced over the summer, with fields and those buildings closed to the public.
  • Supervisor: Jolicoeur said the comprehensive plan update, funded by a Hudson River Valley Greenway grant, would replace a plan from 2007, and invited residents to apply for the steering committee. She also explained that Town Hall stayed open on Juneteenth but let employees leave two hours early, as it does before holidays.
  • Public comment: Council members urged care when charging lithium-ion batteries and encouraged residents to fill out a "File of Life" medical card. Councilman Brooks said a mountain biking association had won state approval to build more than two miles of trails at Canopus Lake in Fahnestock State Park.

Reports were also listed from the Tompkins Corners Cultural Center, the library, the fire department, the Climate Smart Task Force and County Legislator Bill Gouldman, but the minutes do not record their content. The next meeting was a work session on August 12.

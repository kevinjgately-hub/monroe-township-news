Town Board · Written-Records Roundup

What Putnam Valley's Town Board has put on the record this fall: a tax-cap override, two moratoriums and a Verizon deal

The board adopts a law letting the 2027 budget exceed the state tax cap, schedules November 18 hearings on temporary bans on data centers and on large battery-storage and wind projects, and sets a December hearing on a cable deal that could bring Verizon Fios to about 5,000 homes.

Nov 25, 2026 · Compiled from public notices and the Supervisor's newsletters posted on the town website, not from recordings · 6 min read

Quick facts

What
The Town Board's public notices and the Supervisor's newsletters from August to November 2026
Where
Town of Putnam Valley, Putnam County, New York; documents are on putnamvalley.gov
Why a roundup
The newest Town Board minutes the town has posted are from July 15, and the August 19 agenda is a scanned image with no readable text. For September through November, notices and newsletters are the written record available.
How to read this
A notice says what the board planned to take up. Only a notice of adoption or minutes confirm what it did. Where we only have a notice, we say so.

Putnam Valley's five-member Town Board, led by Supervisor Alison Jolicoeur with Council Members Louie Luongo, Sherry Howard, Christian Russo and CJ Brooks, usually holds a 5 p.m. work session on the second Wednesday of the month and a 6 p.m. regular meeting on the third Wednesday. Its meetings are shown on the town's cable channel and posted online, but the minutes the town has put on its website stop in July. (We cover the July 15 meeting and the July 8 work session separately.) This roundup pieces the fall together from what the town has published since.

1. The board adopts a law letting the 2027 budget go over the state tax cap

New York's property tax cap limits how much a town's total tax levy (the amount it raises from property taxes) can grow each year, generally to 2% or the rate of inflation, whichever is lower, with some adjustments. A town can go above that limit only if its board first passes a local law to override the cap. On November 18 the board held a public hearing on Local Law No. 8 of 2026, "A Local Law to Override the Tax Levy Limit Established in General Municipal Law § 3-c for the Fiscal Year 2027." A notice signed by Town Clerk Michelle Stephens and dated that same day says the board adopted it on November 18. The notice gives no vote count.

The override law itself does not tell us whether the final 2027 levy actually exceeds the cap; towns sometimes pass one as a precaution. The Supervisor's October newsletter says the "effective tax rate increase" in the draft budget was brought down from 7.2% to 3.34%, through spending changes in the Highway and Parks departments and lower workers' compensation costs. The preliminary budget the town posted (marked "updated 10-21-2026") is a scanned document we could not read, so we cannot yet report the budget's total, the levy, or how either compares with 2026.

The town's published schedule had the budget presented to the board on September 16, made the "preliminary" budget on October 14, aired at a public hearing on October 21, and adopted, together with the Highway and special district budgets, on November 18. A separate notice also scheduled a special pre-work session on September 30 to present the tentative budget. As of November 25 the town had not posted a notice confirming the budget's adoption.

2. Hearings on temporary bans on data centers, and on large battery-storage and wind projects

On October 22 the clerk posted notices for two more public hearings, both also held November 18 at 6 p.m.:

  • Local Law No. 6 of 2026, "A Local Law Establishing a Temporary Moratorium on Data Centers in the Town of Putnam Valley."
  • Local Law No. 7 of 2026, "A Local Law Establishing a Temporary Land Use Moratorium Prohibiting Large-Scale Battery Energy Storage System Installations and Large-Scale Wind Energy Conversion Systems within the Town of Putnam Valley."

A moratorium is a temporary pause: while it is in effect, the town will not approve the listed kinds of projects, which usually gives the town time to write permanent rules. Neither notice says how long the pause would last, what size of project counts as "large-scale," or why the board is proposing it; those details are in the full text of each law, which the notices say is available at Town Hall. The town has been here before: its online code includes Local Law No. 3 of 2024, an earlier temporary moratorium with nearly the same title covering large battery-storage and wind installations. As of November 25 the town had posted no notice saying whether Local Laws 6 and 7 were adopted.

3. A December 16 hearing on a cable deal with Verizon

The board has scheduled a public hearing for December 16 at 6 p.m. on a proposed cable television franchise agreement with Verizon New York, the contract that would let Verizon offer cable TV over town roads in exchange for fees and conditions. The notice says the hearing will begin once the New York State Public Service Commission confirms the agreement, so the date could move. The October newsletter says about 5,000 Putnam Valley homes would be able to get cable through Verizon Fios and that details were still being negotiated. The application and agreement can be inspected at the Town Clerk's office.

4. After the July storm: an emergency declaration and work on the creeks

The town declared a state of emergency at 4 p.m. on July 29 because of flash flooding, to last 30 days unless lifted sooner. In her August newsletter the Supervisor wrote that 8.22 inches of rain fell in Cold Spring on July 28 and 29, much of it draining into the valley, and that the declaration let the town bring in the state Department of Environmental Conservation, whose team toured damaged areas with Highway Superintendent Shawn Keeler. She wrote that the board passed a resolution allowing the Highway Department to clear storm debris from Canopus Brook and Peekskill Hollow Brook after consulting affected property owners, and calling for a review, with the state, of how water moves through Canopus Creek. Residents with storm damage were asked to fill out a survey on the town website. The newsletter did not give damage totals or mention federal disaster aid.

5. Smaller items

The October newsletter reports that on October 21 the board agreed to a request from VFW Post 391 to skip the traditional Veterans Day ceremony and Town Hall wreaths and steer donations to veterans' groups instead. The Cimarron Road bridge over Canopus Creek, which the board was paying for in installments this summer, reopened after less than three months of construction, according to the August newsletter.

What we don't have

We could not find minutes, readable agendas or vote counts for any Town Board meeting after July 15. The town's video of its meetings is posted on its "Channel 18 & 20" page; we have not yet recapped those recordings. If you need the outcome of a specific vote, the Town Clerk's office (845-526-2121, Town Hall, 265 Oscawana Lake Road) can provide minutes once they are approved.

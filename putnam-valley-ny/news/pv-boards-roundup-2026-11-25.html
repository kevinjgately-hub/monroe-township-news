Putnam Valley's lakes commission, Climate Smart task force and comprehensive-plan committee: what the town has said about them

The Commission of Lakes is weighing algae fixes for Lake Peekskill and wakeboat rules for Lake Oscawana, the town is applying for a three-year study of toxic algae flowing toward Peekskill's water supply, and a resident committee is starting work on the first comprehensive-plan update since 2007.

Nov 25, 2026 · Compiled from the Supervisor's newsletters and Town Board minutes posted on the town website · 5 min read

Quick facts

What
Town advisory bodies other than the Town Board, Planning Board and Zoning Board of Appeals
Sources
None of these bodies posts its own agendas or minutes that we could find. What we know comes from the Supervisor's monthly newsletters and reports made at Town Board meetings.
Open to the public
The town says Comprehensive Plan Steering Committee meetings are open to the public; Commission of Lakes meetings are announced in the newsletter and held at Town Hall

1. The Commission of Lakes takes on algae, wakeboats and water levels

Putnam Valley's lakes are central to the town, and the Supervisor has been reorganizing a Commission of Lakes to coordinate work on them. According to her September newsletter, the commission's September meeting covered:

  • Lake Peekskill: the town's contract with EverBlue Lakes, which runs ultrasonic units meant to control algae, is winding down, and the town is looking at how to keep the units running and keep monitoring the lake.
  • Harmful algal blooms: a representative from Lake Mohegan shared findings on ultrasonic, biological and nutrient-control treatments, and efforts continue to change state law to allow "nutrient inactivants" such as alum, which bind the phosphorus that feeds algae.
  • Roaring Brook Lake: a proposed survey of underwater plants would show how vegetation has recovered since the decline of the lake's grass carp (weed-eating fish) and last year's drawdown, and could support permits to stock more carp in 2027.
  • Lake Oscawana: wakeboats, shoreline erosion and water safety; a proposed map of where wakeboats may operate is being revised, and members discussed a more informed way of managing the lake's valve based on water levels and expected rain.

The commission's November 10 meeting was set to feature a lake restoration consultant revisiting a 2011 survey and restoration design for Lake Peekskill, with a focus on phosphorus, filter materials such as biochar and zeolite, and a possible regional approach with Lake Mohegan and Lake Carmel to compete for state and federal funding. We have not seen a record of what was said.

2. A grant application to track toxic algae toward Peekskill's water

The town is the lead applicant, with the City of Peekskill as a partner, for a competitive grant to fund a three-year study of how toxin-producing algae move from upstream lakes and reservoirs toward Peekskill's drinking-water supply, according to the September newsletter. GEI Consultants and UMass Dartmouth would sample five lakes and four stream locations during algae season, including during storms, and model future risks. The newsletter notes the funding is not guaranteed.

3. The comprehensive plan gets its first update since 2007

A comprehensive plan is a town's long-range guide for land use, open space and infrastructure, and zoning is supposed to follow it. At the July 15 Town Board meeting the Supervisor said Putnam Valley's plan dates to 2007 and that the update is funded by a Hudson River Valley Greenway grant, with topics including stormwater, resiliency, open space, natural resources and development. Her August newsletter said many residents applied to join the Steering Committee, the board expected to finalize it by the end of August with meetings starting in September, and all of its meetings would be open to the public. We have not found posted agendas, minutes or a membership list.

4. Climate Smart Task Force: pumpkins and Earth Week

The Climate Smart Task Force works with the town's Commission for the Conservation of the Environment on Earth Week events and the state's Climate Smart Communities program. Through November 30 it is running its second annual Pumpkin Recycling Month with the Grange and the library: residents can drop pumpkins, with decorations removed, in bins at either location, and they go to food donation or to Cucumber Hill Farm as animal feed. Last year's drive collected more than 1,000 pounds.

Bodies with no posted records

The town's website lists other boards and committees, including the Advisory Board on Architecture & Community Appearance, the Board of Assessment Review, the Board of Ethics, the Parks & Recreation Commission, the Town Safety Committee and the Roaring Brook Lake Preservation Committee. We could not find agendas or minutes for any of them online. That does not mean they are not meeting, only that their records are not posted.

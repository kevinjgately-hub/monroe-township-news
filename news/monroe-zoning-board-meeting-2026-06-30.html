Zoning Board · Regular Meeting

Everything that happened at Monroe's June 30 Zoning Board meeting

A federal security grant is paying for a taller fence around a Sikh house of worship, a long-planned Applegarth Road subdivision clears its last hurdle, and a neighbor's application gets carried to next month after a family death — the short version of a meeting that wrapped in well under an hour.

Meeting held Tuesday, June 30, 2026 · Compiled from the official meeting recording

What: Monroe Township Zoning Board of Adjustment, regular monthly meeting

Where: Monroe Township Municipal Building

When: Tuesday, June 30, 2026, 6:30 p.m.

Who was there: Chairman Vincent LaFata presiding, with most of the board's regular members and three of its four alternates present

This is Monroe Township News's first look at the Zoning Board of Adjustment, so a quick note on what it does: unlike the Planning Board, which reviews larger developments and zoning ordinance changes for consistency with the township's long-term master plan, the Zoning Board hears requests from individual property owners for relief from the township's zoning rules — either a "variance" (permission to do something the zoning code doesn't otherwise allow, like a shorter setback or a taller fence) or, less often, approval of a minor subdivision. It's a quasi-judicial body: applicants and their attorneys present testimony under oath, board members and the township's own attorney, engineer, and planner ask questions, and the public can weigh in before the board votes. June's meeting was a short one by this board's standards, running well under an hour and clearing three applications.

A federal security grant is paying for a taller fence at a Sikh house of worship

Guru Nanak Sikh Heritage, Inc., which operates a Sikh temple, or gurdwara, at 29 Wyckoff Mills Road, asked the board for a bulk variance — relief from a specific numerical zoning limit, as opposed to a use variance, which would let a property be used in a way the zoning doesn't normally allow — to install a 6-foot metal security fence with an automatic sliding gate around the property. The site sits in a residential zone where front-yard fences are normally capped at 3 feet. The applicant's attorney told the board the fence is being funded through a federal grant program that helps houses of worship pay for security upgrades, following what he described as an increase nationally in violence targeting religious institutions; the property's caretaker testified that the site has had issues with vehicles entering during overnight hours when the temple is closed. The board's engineer noted the application was nearly identical to a fence variance the board approved for the Chabad House, a Jewish community center, "a few months ago" — meaning this wasn't the board's first time weighing this exact tradeoff.

The board approved the fence variance unanimously with no public opposition. Because the federal grant funding comes with its own deadline, the applicant's attorney had already prepared the written resolution memorializing the board's decision — normally a separate step handled at a later meeting — and asked the board to adopt it that same night, which the board did, also unanimously.

A long-planned Applegarth Road subdivision moved forward

257 Applegarth LLC returned to the board seeking approval to split a roughly 15-acre property near Applegarth and Union Valley roads into three lots: two for single-family homes and one for a future commercial shopping center. This followed up on a use variance the board granted back in December 2025 that allowed the commercial portion of the site to be zoned for that use; Tuesday's request was for a "minor subdivision" — the formal process of legally dividing one piece of property into multiple separate lots — plus a single "design waiver," a minor exception from a technical site-design rule rather than a full variance. Here, the rule in question requires side lot lines to meet streets at roughly a right angle; the applicant's engineer explained the proposed lot lines instead follow the property's existing tree-line boundary and a stream that runs through the site, which the board's own planner testified was actually the better outcome, since it avoids carving out an oddly-shaped lot along an arbitrary straight line.

Board members' questions focused on practical details: confirming a driveway turnaround would stay the required 5 feet from the property line, and separating which infrastructure improvements would happen now versus later. The applicant committed to installing curbing and sidewalks along Union Valley Road as part of this approval, but asked to defer matching improvements along Applegarth Road until it comes back for a separate, future site-plan approval to actually build the commercial center — since building those improvements now risked their being damaged during that later construction. The board approved the subdivision and the single design waiver, with conservation easements required along the property's wetlands, buffers, and the boundary with an adjacent home.

Also from Tuesday's meeting

An Old Forge Road application was carried to July, after a family death

An application from the DiPasquale family for a variance at 232 Old Forge Road — for an undersized lot — was carried to the board's next meeting at the applicant's request, while the family works out whether it can resolve the issue by buying or selling a small piece of adjoining property. No testimony was taken. During the public comment period later in the meeting, a woman who identified herself as the daughter of the property's former owner, who died in April, said she planned to serve as executor of the estate but had a scheduling conflict with the new date; the board's attorney advised her to put her request in writing to the board secretary. A neighbor across the street separately asked how she'd be notified of the rescheduled hearing so she could attend; she was told to check with the township's planning office, since a further postponement was still possible.

Three previously-approved applications were formally memorialized

The board also adopted the written resolutions documenting three decisions it had already made verbally at earlier meetings — a routine formal step called memorialization that doesn't reflect any new decision — for applications from Tlex Realty, Inc., a property at 557 Franklin Plaza, and one additional applicant from the board's May meeting. All three passed without discussion. The board's next regular meeting is scheduled for July 28, 2026.

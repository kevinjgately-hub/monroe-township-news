Planning Board · Full Meeting Recap

Everything that happened at Monroe's September 24 Planning Board meeting

A short meeting with no new applications: the board puts a wooded subdivision's curb-and-sidewalk waiver in writing, a resident asks about a development near Butcher Road, and the chairman opens with a moment of silence for a longtime township public servant.

Sep 24, 2026 · Compiled from the official meeting recording, published on the township's YouTube channel

Quick facts

What
Monroe Township Planning Board, regular monthly meeting
Where
Monroe Township Municipal Building, Monroe Township, Middlesex County, New Jersey
When
Thursday, September 24, 2026, 6:30 p.m.; the recording runs about 18 minutes
Who was there
Chairman Marc Gaffrey, Vice Chairman David Rothman, the Mayor's representative (Hemant Patel), Councilman Michael Markel and several other members, with the board's quorum confirmed at roll call. Names are as listed on the township's website.

The Planning Board reviews larger development proposals and changes to the township's zoning rules, and it checks them against the township's long-term master plan. Its agenda for September 24 listed no applications at all, only one housekeeping item. That made this one of the shortest meetings we have covered. There was no August meeting (the township's calendar shows it as canceled), so the board's previous meeting was July 23, which we covered here.

1. A wooded subdivision's curb-and-sidewalk waiver is put in writing

The one item of business was a resolution for Greenvest, LLC (file PB-1262-24), a minor subdivision the board has handled before. As we reported in July, the board originally approved the subdivision in 2023 and affirmed it in 2024, then approved it again after a legal filing deadline was missed while the property changed hands. A "minor subdivision" is a small division of land into a few lots that doesn't need the full site-plan review a big development does. At the July meeting, the board agreed to waive the normal requirements to install curbs and sidewalks, along with the related fee, and the chairman recalled a lengthy discussion in which it was felt that waiving both was appropriate in this case. The board's attorney at the time made the point that each application is judged individually, so the waiver would not set a precedent for future applications.

Because a board's decision isn't official until it is written down and adopted, the September meeting was where that happened. The board's attorney told members it was their resolution to adopt, and a member moved to adopt it with the correction about the curbs. After a second, the board voted in favor on a roll call. Every vote the captions captured, including those of the vice chairman and Councilman Markel, was a yes.

2. A resident asks about a development near Butcher Road

During the public portion, a Monroe resident asked about construction the resident described as ongoing on Butcher Road and a nearby road, where townhouses and affordable ("low-income") housing were said to be going up. (The captions garble the second road's name.) The resident said the only notice received was a certified-mail letter about a wetlands matter. A board member said there was no pending application for it that they knew of, and the resident was told to contact the township's planning and zoning department the next day to see whether an application has been filed and to look at the file. One member added that it sounded very early in whatever process is under way. A board member reminded the resident that the department exists to represent the public and that residents should feel free to talk to them.

Also from Thursday's meeting

A moment of silence for a longtime public servant

After the pledge of allegiance, the chairman asked the room to remain standing for a moment of silence for a man who served Monroe in many roles over the years, among them as a zoning board member, planning board member, councilman, director of planning and zoning, director of environmental protection and cultural arts commissioner. He said the man died on Monday, September 21, in Arizona. The captions are unclear on the name, so we are not printing it here; the township's own announcement or the recording will have it.

July minutes approved

The board approved the minutes of its July 23 meeting by voice vote. The chairman noted the board did not meet in August. The next regular meeting is scheduled for October 22, 2026.

Planning Board · Agenda Preview

What was on Monroe's Planning Board agenda for July 23

The old landfill's redevelopment comes back before the board, along with a zoning rewrite touching four commercial and industrial districts — but this is a preview of what was scheduled, not a report on what the board actually did.

Meeting held July 23, 2026 · Compiled from the board's official published agenda

Why this story is different: Every other story on this site is built from the full video or transcript of the meeting, so we can tell you what was actually said and decided. For this meeting, that wasn't possible: the township's YouTube video of this Planning Board meeting has no usable captions or transcript, and the board's written minutes haven't been posted (its most recent posted minutes are from January 2025, a year and a half behind). So what follows is a preview of the published agenda only — what the board planned to discuss — not a recap of votes, discussion, or outcomes. We'll update or follow up if that changes.

The Monroe Township Planning Board is the body that reviews specific development proposals — new buildings, subdivided lots, changes to how a property can be used — to make sure they follow the township's zoning rules, and it also has to formally weigh in on proposed zoning ordinance changes before the town council can adopt them, to make sure those changes line up with the township's long-term master plan. The board's agenda for July 23 was set for 6:30 p.m. at the municipal building, with nine regular and alternate members listed: Mayor's Representative Hemant Patel, Councilman Michael Markel, Chairman Marc Gaffrey, Vice Chairman David Rothman, and members Greg Slavicek, Roslyn Brodsky, Manish Patel, and Carol Damiani, plus alternates Jay Weiner and Howard Butensky.

The old landfill's redevelopment plan was back on the agenda

Monroe's council voted on August 3 to rezone the township's old landfill — a former town dump turned federal Superfund site — to allow only a large community solar farm, as part of settling a long-running lawsuit against the waste company responsible for the site (see our August 3 council recap for the full background). Nearly a month earlier, on this July 23 Planning Board agenda, a proposed ordinance — numbered O-6-2026-014 — covering that same Municipal Landfill Redevelopment Plan was listed as a discussion item, covering specific parcels of the landfill property (identified on tax maps as Block 148, Lots 36.01 and 37, and Block 148.60, Lots 23.01, 24, 25, 26, and 27). This is very likely an early step in the same process that led to August's council vote and September's expected follow-up vote on a purchase agreement with the solar company — planning boards typically review this kind of zoning change before it goes to the full council — but without a transcript or minutes, we can't confirm what the board actually discussed or recommended that night.

A separate ordinance would rewrite rules for four commercial and industrial zones

Also listed for discussion was a proposed ordinance, O-6-2026-013, amending zoning rules across four types of districts: PO/CD (Planned Office Commercial), H-D (Highway Development), L-I (Light Industrial), and OP (Office Professional). The agenda doesn't specify what the amendments would change. Zoning changes like this typically govern things like what kinds of businesses can operate in a district, building size and setback rules, or parking requirements — but again, the specifics of what's being proposed, and what the board thinks of it, aren't available without the meeting's discussion.

A few other items were on the agenda

Two previously-reviewed development applications — one from Kings Row Developers, LLC and one from 30 Abeel Road SG, LLC — were listed for "memorialization," the formal step where a board adopts the written resolution documenting a decision it already made verbally at an earlier meeting; this doesn't necessarily mean anything new was decided on July 23 itself. A third item, from Greenvest, LLC, asked the board to "reaffirm" a minor subdivision it had approved previously. The board's next regular meeting is scheduled for August 27, 2026.

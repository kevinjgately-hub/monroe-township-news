Town Council · Full Meeting Recap

Everything that happened at Monroe's January 5 council meeting

Rupa Siegel is elected council president on a 4–1 vote and Michael Markel vice president, three longtime employees retire, the council moves a planned solar site toward a redevelopment plan as a resident questions the public notice, and the new police dispatch center opens.

Jan 5, 2026 · Compiled from the official meeting recording, published on the township's YouTube channel

Quick facts

What
Monroe Township Council reorganization meeting, followed by a combined agenda and regular meeting
Where
Monroe Township, Middlesex County, New Jersey
When
Monday, January 5, 2026; the recording runs about 50 minutes. The reorganization meeting adjourned at 6:41 p.m., the agenda session at 7:04 p.m., and the regular meeting at 7:26 p.m.
Who was there
Councilwoman Miriam Cohen, Councilman Charles Dipierro, Councilman Terence Van Dzura, Council Vice President Michael Markel and Council President Rupa Siegel were all present, along with Business Administrator Kevin McGowan. Mayor Stephen Dalina was absent, so his report was skipped.

Every January a New Jersey town council holds a reorganization meeting, where it picks its leaders for the year or, in Monroe's case, for two years. Monroe then held its usual two-part meeting on the same night: an agenda session, where the council reads through the items and residents can ask about them, and a regular meeting, where the votes happen. An ordinance is a permanent local law that must be voted on twice: first reading is the introduction, and second reading, after a public hearing, is the vote to adopt. A resolution is a one-time official decision, and routine ones are bundled into a single vote called the consent agenda.

1. Rupa Siegel becomes council president and Michael Markel vice president

The council chose its leaders for the two-year term that runs from January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027. Councilwoman Rupa Siegel was elected council president by a vote of 4–1, with Councilman Dipierro casting the no vote; he did not explain it on the record. The council then elected Councilman Michael Markel vice president unanimously, on a nomination from Councilman Van Dzura. Each was sworn in at the dais, and Siegel took over the meeting. The previous council president, Councilwoman Miriam Cohen, was thanked by several people, including Siegel, who credited her leadership and mentorship over the past few years, and by a resident in the public comment period.

In her opening remarks the new president thanked her colleagues for their trust and pledged to work with the mayor, the administration and residents. Markel thanked Van Dzura for the nomination and for his work in the role, and joked that he was "a heartbeat away" from Siegel's seat. Councilman Dipierro, who is beginning another four-year term, asked that any response to a council member's question be sent to all council members, not a few, after a disagreement about whether a survey had been distributed to everyone; the administrator said he would resend it to everyone the next day. Councilman Van Dzura said 2025 had been a year of change, including the departure of the previous business administrator, and praised Business Administrator McGowan, who stepped into the job.

2. Three veteran employees retire, and promotions fill their jobs

The township honored three retiring employees in the agenda session, with plaques thanking each for going "above and beyond" in service to residents. One is an EMS employee with 34 years who could not attend. Another began in 1990 at the Monroe Township Utility Authority and built its purchasing department over 35 years; he thanked colleagues and said he looked forward to time on the beach and with his granddaughter. The third is the tax collector, who started as a tax clerk in October 2008, earned her certified tax collector's license, became tax collector in October 2024 and retires after 17 years.

Business Administrator McGowan said both jobs will be filled by longtime township employees who are new to the roles but not to the township. The council also proclaimed January 27, 2026 International Holocaust Remembrance Day and January 2026 Muslim Heritage Month.

3. A planned solar site moves toward a redevelopment plan, and a resident questions the notice

Resolution 22, part of the consent agenda, declared certain properties "an area in need of redevelopment" under New Jersey's local redevelopment law and directed the planning board to prepare a redevelopment plan. That is the formal step that lets a town set special rules for a site and work with a developer on it. In the agenda session, a resident asked about the resolution, and Council President Siegel said the properties will become a solar field, that the planning board's study of the site is finished, and that the next step is a redevelopment plan describing what the solar field will look like. The council later approved the consent agenda, 5–0. (A redevelopment plan ordinance for a landfill solar site was introduced on June 29; see our June 29 recap.)

A resident commented that the planning board had made its December 18, 2025 finding at a meeting where the resident said they were nearly the only member of the public present. The resident said the board's agenda listed no applications, with the item shown only lower down as a discussion item on which "action may be taken," and argued that this was not meaningful notice, might not meet the Open Public Meetings Act, and fell short of transparency. The resident also asked the council to deliberate in public and to describe their vision for Monroe's future. The township attorney responded after public comment closed that the item was clearly listed as a discussion item that could lead to action, that he saw nothing in how the agenda was structured that violated the law, and that the designation was made at a properly noticed public meeting. He added that New Jersey is phasing out printed newspaper legal notices, so notices will be easier to find in one place on the township website and a state site.

Also from Monday's meeting

Ordinance introduced and consent items

The council introduced Ordinance 01-2026-0001, the 2026 salary and wage ordinance for supervisory personnel, on a 5–0 vote; it was adopted at the February 9 meeting (see our February 9 recap). Resolutions 3 through 30 were bundled into the consent agenda, with three (resolutions 8, 16 and 19) pulled and voted on separately; all passed.

Questions on three resolutions

Residents asked about several consent items in the agenda session. Resolution 10, they were told, authorizes the business administrator to sign applications for federal surplus property each year; there is no dollar limit, but any actual purchase would still have to come to the council. Resolution 20 puts a landlocked parcel between Hoffman Road and Federal Road, which the county bought as preserved open space, on the tax-exempt list and refunds the prepaid taxes, which officials said came to a little over $4,000. A third resolution appoints the administrator and two alternates as the township's commissioners on the Garden State Municipal Joint Insurance Fund, a pool of towns that buys insurance together. Officials said Monroe left the Middlesex County fund about five or six years ago after that fund ran into financial trouble, that the township's current commitment runs through the end of 2027, and that last year's assessment was about $1.7 million, covering workers' compensation, property, liability, law enforcement and cyber coverage; the 2026 rate was still being negotiated.

Swearing-in ceremony

At the start of the agenda session, a resident said they were disappointed that the public could not watch the swearing-in of the three reelected council members and asked where it had been noticed. Officials said the ceremony was a ceremonial function rather than a public meeting under the Open Public Meetings Act, so it was not noticed the way meetings are, but that it was open to the public and the room was full. Dipierro later thanked the mayor and the clerk's office for it.

New police dispatch center

Asked about the police station project, the business administrator said that police dispatchers have moved into the new dispatch center, that the council approved furniture for the rest of the staff to move in, that the sprinkler system is nearly done, and that the spray insulation in the attic is finished. The building has a temporary certificate of occupancy, which lets it be used while minor work is still underway, but not a permanent one, and officials have no date for it. The remaining work is outside, such as site work and parking, plus floors, walls and finishes in the older part of the building, and the township will work with the bond company through the spring to finish it.

Recreation survey, school and reports

The administrator said the township's open space and recreation survey is complete: residents' top requests were hiking and bike trails, volleyball and more playgrounds for younger children. The engineer reported nothing new, because of the cold and the holidays. Councilman Dipierro said Applegarth School has reopened after repairs to its boiler and roof, which the township helped pay for, and mentioned an Eagle Scout who built a storage cart for tables and chairs for a local congregation. Council President Siegel said about 20 students are in this year's youth advisory council, which teaches municipal government and includes a community service project, and that the cultural arts commission's juried art show runs at the library from January 10 through the end of February. The next council meeting was set for February 9 at 6:30 p.m.

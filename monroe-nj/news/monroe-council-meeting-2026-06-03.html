Town Council · Full Meeting Recap

Everything that happened at Monroe's June 3 council meeting

The council approves construction of a roughly $3.2 million indoor sports building at James Monroe Park amid questions about the police station expansion, officials say there are no data centers or data center applications in town, and the engineer maps out a summer of paving and a stalled county intersection.

Jun 3, 2026 · Compiled from the official meeting recording, published on the township's YouTube channel · 9 min read

Quick facts

What
Monroe Township Council, combined agenda & regular meeting
Where
Monroe Township, Middlesex County, New Jersey
When
Wednesday, June 3, 2026; the recording runs about 1 hour 4 minutes, and the agenda session adjourned at 6:56 p.m. before the regular meeting began
Who was there
Councilwoman Miriam Cohen, Councilman Charles Dipierro, Councilman Terence Van Dzura, Council Vice President Michael Markel, Council President Rupa Siegel, Mayor Stephen Dalina and Business Administrator Kevin McGowan.

Monroe's council meets in two parts on the same night. The agenda session comes first: the council reads the list of items, residents can ask questions about anything on it, and nothing controversial is usually decided. The regular meeting follows, with the formal votes and reports. Routine resolutions (one-time official decisions) are bundled into a single vote called the consent agenda unless a council member asks for one to be pulled out for separate discussion. At this meeting resolutions 111 through 123 were on the consent agenda, none were pulled, and they passed with the votes we could hear all in favor. There were no new ordinances (permanent local laws) on the agenda.

1. The council funds a new indoor sports building, and residents press on how the town protects itself

Two of the 13 consent resolutions, together worth about $2.5 million, were for a new building at James Monroe Park: a contract of about $2.4 million for construction and a flat $87,500 for the township's engineering firm to manage and inspect the work. The business administrator described the project as an 80-by-100-foot multi-use building with indoor turf. It will go in a different spot in the park and will not replace any fields or other amenities, and it is meant for township recreation programs rather than commercial use, with baseball getting priority scheduling because of its location. He put the total cost, including design and construction, at about $3.2 million, with about $160,000 already spent on engineering and design, and said the project is being paid for mostly with state and county grants and a smaller amount of township money. The contractor was the lowest qualified bidder of 12, he said, and has worked with the township's construction manager on other projects. Two residents asked what the $87,500 buys, since the contractor already has its own project manager. The administrator explained that a construction manager works for the township, not the builder, inspecting the work against specifications and code and keeping it on time and on budget, that the fee is a flat not-to-exceed amount rather than an hourly estimate, and that the township would have to come back to the council to spend more.

The harder questions came from the township's recent experience with the expansion of the police station, a project that a resident said is years late with roof leaks. The business administrator said the township fired the general contractor on that job after trying to work with it, and that the contractor's performance bond, a kind of insurance the builder must buy, has now paid for a new general contractor to finish the work at the bond company's expense. That work was to begin within a couple of weeks and be done between summer and early fall. He said this project carries the same bond protection, that the township's construction manager has worked with this contractor before, and that the contractor will be reviewed daily and weekly. Asked whether the township has recovered anything from the architects' or engineers' insurance on the police station job, he answered that it has not. A council member then asked how long the new contract runs and what happens if the contractor misses its deadline. The business administrator said the end date is the end of 2026 and that he believed the penalty, known as liquidated damages, is $500 a day, which he said he would double-check. The same council member, citing the constant questions residents ask about when the work at James Monroe Park will be finished, suggested the township post expected completion dates for projects on its website.

2. "There are no data centers in Monroe," and a question about what law says so

Data centers, the large server buildings that power cloud and artificial intelligence services, had become a regular topic at council meetings, and three officials addressed them on the record. The business administrator said that no data centers are approved anywhere in the township, that there are no applications for one on file in the planning and zoning office, and that they are not a permitted use in any zone. The mayor said the same, and added that he knows residents hold real concerns from several perspectives. The township, he said, has identified studies, is looking at how other New Jersey towns have handled the issue, and will watch the safeguards the state develops, but he also said that false information has been crowding out the facts.

A resident came to the public comment session to ask for the legal basis. If data centers are not a permitted use, the resident asked, which ordinance would let the planning and zoning boards turn down an application? The resident said other towns are passing new ordinances and noted that data centers affect everyone's electric bills even when they are built elsewhere. The business administrator answered that no single ordinance covers the whole township: each zone lists what may be built there, and a developer has to fit within one of those permitted uses, which he called the first hurdle any applicant faces. That answer set up the next meeting. On June 29 the council introduced an ordinance to remove older "data processing" language from four zones so there would be no question left, which we cover in our June 29 recap.

3. Summer road work, a stalled county intersection, and a water tower back in service

The township engineer reported that the five pickleball courts and the parking lot at the senior center were due to be surface-coated and striped the following week, with the project finished in about two weeks, and that the new electric service at the James Monroe Park concession stand needed about another week of work, with completion expected by the end of the month. On Lynx Drive, where a state Department of Transportation grant of about $552,000 is paying for the work, the concrete is finished, and the paving was being held until school let out to reduce traffic. The 2026 township road program, covering about 18 roads, had begun with concrete work, with milling and paving planned for July and August. On the county side, the Prospect Plains Road and Applegarth Road intersection, where lanes are closed, is held up because the county is waiting on revised utility layouts from the power company so it can move poles that conflict with underground lines. The county also planned night work, from 7 p.m. to 5 a.m. from Tuesday, June 9 through Friday, June 12, to mill and pave the Halfacre Road and Forsgate Drive intersection on the Jamesburg border, with road closures.

A resident told the council that an inspector from the county had said the Prospect Plains and Applegarth intersection would not be finished this calendar year because of the power company, called it a hazard with traffic patterns that change daily and frequent police activity, and urged the township to press the utility. The business administrator did not respond on the record. Councilman Dipierro asked when an inspection of the township's traffic signals that can turn lights red for approaching fire trucks and ambulances would be finished. The business administrator said a contractor has been hired to inspect all of them in the township and that he had not yet gotten the report. He said the signal outside the municipal building is not working, and that a beacon and a light pole are down in the median on Center Drive. He also asked about the Route 522 water tower, and the answer was good news: the tank has been painted inside and out, its foundation has been reinforced, the project came in on budget, and the tank would be filled and back in service within a week. Separately, the business administrator thanked the water and sewer utility's staff for working overnight and on holiday weekends through a series of complicated water main breaks over the previous several weeks.

Also from Wednesday's meeting

Proclamations and a presentation

The council proclaimed Flag Day (June 14) and marked a resident's 100th birthday on June 6. It also recognized the Mayor's Youth Advisory Committee: each year the mayor and council president choose 16 students from more than 300 applicants, and the students meet monthly with township leaders and department heads to learn how local government works. This year's final project, in partnership with the township's Historic Preservation Commission, was a history fair for America's 250th anniversary held the following Sunday, June 7, from 1 to 4 p.m. at the Dey Farm site.

Fill trucks on Route 33

A resident said dump trucks were bringing a steady stream of fill into a development site on Route 33, asked whether the fill comes from a tested and approved source with proper paperwork, and said work on utilities and roads was going ahead without inspections and permits. The township engineer said none of that is accurate: the site is being inspected, it has an approved site plan, the developer is providing documentation certifying that the material is clean, and the question of how much fill would be brought in was discussed at length when the planning board approved the project. We have not independently verified either side's account.

Where a new neighborhood recreation facility stands

A resident asked about a possible recreation facility near the golf course and about playground space in the new housing near Route 33. Council President Siegel said the idea is to partner with the Board of Education to build an additional recreation facility on school district land in the Applegarth, Cranberry and Halfacre Road area, but that nothing has been engineered or designed yet and that it is several years away.

Affordability and school costs

A resident used the public comment period to say that the cost of employee health insurance in the school budget, which the resident put at $85,000 per employee, is not affordable, and criticized national politics. The council did not respond.

Housekeeping

The council approved the payment of claims (the monthly bill-paying vote, with a run date of May 28) and the minutes of its May 4 meeting.

Council and mayor's remarks

Councilman Dipierro thanked the clerk's office for running the primary election held the day before, mentioned the banner ceremony for the Monroe High School varsity ice hockey team's back-to-back conference championships and asked for a plan for the community center hockey rink once the parking lot design is ready (an issue that came back at the June 29 meeting), attended a tribute at the community garden named for a late Shade Tree Commission member, Joel Greenberg, and noted that the senior chorus of 26 singers rehearses Tuesdays at 9 a.m. at the senior center. Councilman Van Dzura remembered a 39-year-old Monroe resident and state corrections officer who died suddenly the week before, leaving a wife and two young children, and mentioned that a fundraising page had been set up, then congratulated the high school baseball team, seeded ninth, on winning the Middlesex County Tournament over Old Bridge, 7–0, its first title since 2015, and the boys volleyball team on a 19–1 regular season. Councilwoman Cohen announced free mahjong gatherings at the library on June 24 and July 14 and pointed residents to the Parks and Recreation bulletin for July 4 week events beginning June 29. Vice President Markel praised the high school's 10th annual Armed Forces Appreciation Day, said he had looked up the names and families of the 13 service members he said have died in the current conflict with Iran and thought the town should know who they were, and invited residents to the township's Juneteenth celebration on June 18 at the senior center. Council President Siegel said Monroe middle school students ranked among the top 10 schools in the county for donations to a food collection that gathered more than 77,000 pounds this school year, and announced Thursday concerts at 6 p.m. in Thompson Park from July 9 through August 13, along with farmers markets starting soon at the library.

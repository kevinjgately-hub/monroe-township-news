Zoning Board · Full Meeting Recap

Everything that happened at Monroe's October 27 Zoning Board meeting

The board turns down a house on a 5,000-square-foot lot on Old Forge Road bought through a tax lien, approves a new home lot in front of a mulching operation on Spotswood-Englishtown Road and a bigger deck in Stratford, and puts its September approvals in writing.

Oct 27, 2026 · Compiled from the official meeting recording, published on the township's YouTube channel · 6 min read

Quick facts

What
Monroe Township Zoning Board of Adjustment, regular monthly meeting
Where
Monroe Township Municipal Building, Monroe Township, Middlesex County, New Jersey
When
Tuesday, October 27, 2026, 6:30 p.m.; the livestream runs about 1 hour 38 minutes, with the meeting starting about 6 minutes in
Who was there
Vice Chairman Louis Masters ran the meeting; the chairman did not answer at roll call. Seven members voted on the first applications. The captions garble most members' names, so we are not listing the rest.

The Zoning Board of Adjustment hears property owners' requests to depart from the zoning rules, whether a "use" variance to use land in a way the zone doesn't allow or a "bulk" variance to bend a measurement rule such as a setback or lot size. Applicants' experts testify under oath, the board's own engineer and planner weigh in, the public can speak, and the board votes. No agenda for this meeting was posted on the township's Zoning Board web page, but the meeting was held and livestreamed as scheduled. Here's what happened, biggest stories first.

1. A house on a tiny Old Forge Road lot is denied

The board turned down a request to build a three-bedroom house with a one-car garage at 232 Old Forge Road (Block 106.88, Lots 5 and 6), two lots totaling just 5,000 square feet (50 by 100 feet) in the R-60 zone, where the minimum lot is 60,000 square feet. The owner testified that the lots date to a 1910 subdivision that sold small parcels to buyers from far away, that she acquired them through a tax lien, a foreclosure process she said began in 2002, and that she had paid taxes on the property for about 25 years. She said the owner of the surrounding wooded land, now an estate, would neither sell her more land nor buy hers, and that a two-year listing with a realtor found no buyer.

Besides the existing undersized lot, the plan needed variances for front, side and rear setbacks and for building and impervious coverage: 28.5% and 36.9% where the R-60 zone allows 20% and 30%. The applicant's engineer said a dry well would hold more than the extra stormwater the house would create, that the site has no wetlands or flood-hazard areas, and that the design would meet every setback in the township's R-10 zone. Its planner said it would comply with the R-5 zone's rules, that the house would be about 80 feet from the one neighboring home, and that an evergreen buffer would be planted. He argued the lot's size is a hardship the owner can't cure and that without the variances it would be unusable. The board's engineer asked for a larger turnaround in the driveway and clearer sight-triangle dimensions, which the applicant agreed to.

A neighbor across the street said Old Forge Road traffic has grown substantially, that turning in and out near Pine View Court is already dangerous, and that the board should not approve a house on a lot so far below the zone's minimum. Under questioning, the owner said she bought the lien as an investment, expecting the land to gain value or a neighbor to want it, and that she now planned to build the house and then rent or sell it. The member who moved to deny the application said she had bought the property as an investment and "lost," comparing it to a stock that goes down, and said he was sorry for her. The motion to deny carried; every member whose vote the captions caught voted for denial except one, who abstained.

2. A new home lot in front of a mulching operation is approved

The board approved carving a roughly 30,700-square-foot residential lot from a 34.8-acre property on Spotswood-Englishtown Road (County Route 613; Block 53, Lot 15.09) in the R-60 zone, filed on behalf of an estate. The rest of the property is a mulching operation the board approved years ago with a use variance, so any change there has to come back to this board. The board's planner said the request is technically an amended use variance. The new lot needed variances for lot area (about 30,700 square feet where 60,000 is required) and for lot width and frontage (141.87 feet where 200 is required), and the remaining property's frontage shrinks to about 40 feet, where an existing dirt road will stay as a secondary access.

The applicant's planner said five neighboring lots along the road, four of them with homes, are about the same size, and that one more house would complete the row of homes in front of the mulching business and further hide it. The mulching operation's access and activity won't change, he said. The applicant agreed to put in curb and sidewalk as the county directed. The board's planner recommended, but did not require, a landscaped buffer between the new house and the mulching operation; the approval left it optional. The vote was 7–0.

3. A larger deck in Stratford is approved

A homeowner in the Stratford community won approval to replace a small 5-by-10-foot builder's deck with a 16-by-26-foot composite deck that comes within about 18.8 feet of the rear property line, where Stratford's zoning requires 25. The applicant's planner said the curved, oddly shaped lot backs onto one of Stratford's largest stormwater basins, with township-preserved woods beyond it, so there are no neighbors behind, and that the deck is well within the size limits the zoning sets for decks. The board's professionals had no objections, no one from the public spoke, and the vote was 7–0.

Also from Tuesday's meeting

One application postponed

Application BA-5273-26 (Block 148, Lot 29.18) was carried to the board's December 1 meeting with no further notice required, at the applicant's request, because, his lawyer said, the attorney handling it was delayed on a flight over the Atlantic.

September's approvals are put in writing

The board adopted the formal written resolutions, called memorializations, for two approvals from its September 29 meeting: Recoil New Jersey's application to sell firearms and ammunition at its shooting range (BA-5271-26) and The Verde Group LLC's application (BA-5274-26), the Chipotle on Route 33. Both passed without discussion. The board also approved its September 29 minutes.

Coming up

The board has no November meeting. Its next meeting is Tuesday, December 1, 2026, at 6:30 p.m.

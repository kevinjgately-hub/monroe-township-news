Board of Education · Full Meeting Recap

Everything that happened at Monroe's September 16 Board of Education meeting

The board approves a three-year teachers' contract with raises of 4.5%, 4% and 4%, formally invokes the "doctrine of necessity" so members with conflicts can vote on matters involving the superintendent, and hears a heated argument over how a law firm was hired.

Sep 16, 2026 · Compiled from the official meeting recording, published on the school district's YouTube channel · 9 min read

Quick facts

What
Monroe Township Board of Education, regular monthly public meeting (after a closed session)
Where
Monroe Township, Middlesex County, New Jersey
When
Wednesday, September 16, 2026; the public recording runs about 1 hour 43 minutes
Who was there
Vice President Carmen Alvarez, who ran the meeting, and members Vanessa Forst, Eric Marozine, Peter Piro, Usha Sharma, Peter Tufano and Charles Zeichner, plus Jamesburg representative Dr. Amanda Peck. Board President Gail DiPane and member Kate Rattner were absent for personal reasons. Acting Superintendent Dr. Adam Layman and Business Administrator and Board Secretary Laura Allen presented.

This was the board's first regular meeting since the start of the school year, and it combined routine reports with some of the most contentious business we have seen this year. Our August 19 recap covers the board's vote to investigate possible tenure charges against Superintendent Dr. Chari Chanley, who remains on paid leave. Here is everything that happened, biggest stories first.

1. The board approves a new three-year teachers' contract

After a closed session, the board came back and voted 8–0 on a roll call to approve a memorandum of agreement with the Monroe Township Education Association (the teachers' union) for July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2029. The vice president called it "a very good contract," and thanked board members Peter Tufano and Vanessa Forst, who served on the negotiating committee. The terms were not read out when the vote was taken, so a resident asked about them during public comment. Business Administrator Laura Allen said the raises are 4.5% in the first year, 4% in the second and 4% in the third. Asked about a separate "instructional rate," Dr. Layman said it rises 2% in the first year and then holds steady for the next two.

The cost of the raises, Allen said, will be offset by changes to health insurance. The district's older "Direct 10" and "Direct 15" plans will still be offered, but they will no longer be the "base plan." The base plan becomes the state's New Jersey Educator Health Benefits Plan, and staff who stay in an older plan will pay the difference in premium. Dr. Layman described the negotiations as "smooth and bumpy" and said both the district and the union felt good about where they ended up. Health-care costs also came up elsewhere in the night: administrators called them the number-one budget pressure for the district and for nearly every district in the state.

2. "Doctrine of necessity" resolutions and a sharp dispute over hiring a law firm

Two resolutions invoking the "doctrine of necessity" were read into the record and approved. The idea is simple: under New Jersey's School Ethics Act, a board member with a conflict of interest generally shouldn't vote on that matter, but if so many members are conflicted that the board can't reach the number of votes it needs to act, the district's policy lets the conflicted members vote anyway, so the board can function. The district's policy says the board must announce this publicly, post the resolution for 30 days and send a copy to the School Ethics Commission. The first resolution covered votes concerning Dr. Chanley's employment. It said six board members had conflicts: some because of litigation involving Dr. Chanley, and one because a spouse is a teacher employed by the district. The second covered paying the bills of the law firm that the board's insurer, the New Jersey Schools Insurance Group, assigned to defend board members in litigation. It said five members were conflicted because they were represented by that firm or were involved in litigation it is defending. Both passed on roll calls; Mr. Tufano was the one "no" vote the captions captured on each.

The topic returned in the "other board business" part of the meeting, when Mr. Tufano raised concerns about how a special law firm was hired to look into possible tenure charges against Dr. Chanley. (Tenure charges are the formal legal process required before a tenured New Jersey educator can be dismissed.) He asked why the board wasn't given three firms and prices to choose from, as it had been when it picked the lawyer behind an earlier investigative report, and who had picked the firm. The vice president answered that the firm was recommended "on advice of defense counsel." Ms. Allen said she had not gathered multiple quotes and that the request to put the item on the agenda came "under the direction of the board president," relayed through the vice president. Another member noted that the full board had voted unanimously to hire the firm. Mr. Tufano and others said conflicted members should not have directed the process; another member called that view "your opinion." The board's attorney confirmed that conflicted members may vote once the doctrine has been invoked, and it was acknowledged that the email in question was sent before the resolution was read. He repeatedly advised members not to discuss privileged legal advice or a personnel matter that, he said, is the subject of several lawsuits in different courts. One member said he had written permission from the superintendent to discuss it; the attorney advised against it regardless. Several members then traded sharp remarks, and the questions about who called the firm and when a prior report was shared with it were not answered on the record. We report this as it was said and take no position on the dispute.

3. Mill Lake School's roof and HVAC are the next big capital project, and a resident says the budget discussion fell short

Under the heading "budget philosophy," administrators told the board how they will approach next year's budget: keep current programs as the baseline, plan for the capital projects ahead, and keep an eye on health-care costs. The next large capital project is the roof and rooftop heating-and-cooling units at Mill Lake School. Administrators said the two jobs should be done together, because replacing a roof and then cutting into it for new units a year later causes problems. The building is laid out in wings, so the work can be done in halves or quarters depending on available money. The first step is paying an architect to design the project so it can be built in the summer of 2027, with two roof types to be priced (one cheaper and shorter-lived, the other costlier and longer-lasting). A board member asked whether the district could finance the work with a roofer on a payment plan; Ms. Allen said equipment such as rooftop units can be lease-purchased, while larger financing would likely require a voter referendum, and the board's attorney said he knew of no law that forbids financing if the bid specifications allow it. The board also discussed its four goals for 2026-27: carrying out the district's "Portrait of a Graduate" (a description of what a Monroe graduate should know and be able to do), legislative advocacy, a thorough review of district policies, and capital planning and finances.

During public comment, a resident who praised the teachers' contract said the budget discussion was "zero discussion" by the board, that it is the board's job to tell administrators what it wants in the budget (for example, whether to stay under a 2% increase), and that school taxes have risen sharply in the last two years. Another resident later argued the opposite on taxes. She said voters approved a permanent increase of about $2.8 million above the regular tax levy in April 2024, that this year's pressure comes largely from health-care costs that are hitting districts statewide, and that Monroe added full-day kindergarten without cutting staff or programs, unlike some neighboring districts she named. A board member responded that he disagreed that the board is in "chaos" and said the district is a model for several programs.

Also from Wednesday's meeting

Phones: "a noticeable difference"

The student board representatives told the board that the new state ban on student cell phones is going well, that students seem to be talking to each other more, and that more work is getting done in class. Dr. Layman said that about a week and a half into the year there had been four first-step disciplinary cases among roughly 2,500 high schoolers. The first step involves warnings and a parent picking up the phone. He said medical needs are handled through medical plans, 504 plans and IEPs, and that elementary students' phones stay turned off in backpacks. The policy committee reported that the district is trying to find a way for students to listen to music without breaking the policy, which has been hard because many music apps have chat features.

Back-to-school news from every school

The student representatives, a returning senior and a newly elected junior, gave school-by-school reports. Highlights: Barkley Brook welcomed its first full-day kindergarten classes; Brookside's third-graders arrived after the district's grade realignment; Mill Lake welcomed more than 100 new kindergarten families and will run a pasta drive with the Monroe ShopRite; Applegarth honored its security guard, a retired NYPD sergeant who responded on 9/11; and at the high school, boys soccer coach Steve McKenzie earned his 400th career win on September 5, which the report said makes him the 35th coach in New Jersey history to do so.

Committee reports

The buildings and grounds committee reported a snow-plow contract renewal at no increase, an unchanged pest-management plan, a $9,563 state grant for a water fountain and bottle-filling station at the stadium fieldhouse, and that the removal of an underground oil tank at the Applegarth School grant project found no contamination. It also said solar installations at several schools are on schedule and that Mill Lake's traffic pattern has been revised to move parent drop-off to the larger bus loop. The legislative advocacy committee discussed delayed state test scores, school-funding changes, working toward a state school-board association certification, and adding board liaisons to groups such as the Cultural Arts Commission and the Commission on Aging. The curriculum committee reviewed new offerings including a computer-science and robotics module using VEX 123 robots, a "Morning Show" TV-production course, Culinary 2, Drama and Acting 2 and Italian 2. The finance committee asked the board to accept a $24,465 state grant that pays Monroe teachers to teach STEM classes at non-public schools outside regular hours; the district is reimbursed, so there is no cost to it beyond processing.

Personnel, board actions and the bill list

The board approved its personnel resolutions and board-action items. Mr. Marozine asked about "17% additional contracts" (Dr. Layman said that is the pay for a teacher who picks up an extra class section, 17% of salary for an extra high-school block, less at lower grades) and abstained on the personnel items, saying he hadn't been able to attend the personnel committee; he and the vice president then traded words about whether he should have contacted her by text or by board email. The monthly bill list was approved except for checks 17670 through 17673, which several members voted against; the recording does not say who those checks were payable to. Mr. Marozine recused himself on bills tied to one law group, and Ms. Forst abstained on the supplemental bill list. In response to Mr. Marozine, Ms. Allen said the Applegarth School roof project has a $250,000 allowance, with about $57,151 billed so far and about $192,849 unused, and that a resolution to submit the Mill Lake and Woodland boiler-replacement plans to the state is about getting plan approval first, with $98,000 budgeted this year as the captions read.

Superintendent's report

Dr. Layman, who gave the combined superintendent and assistant superintendent report, said the first days of full-day kindergarten went well. He announced that the district would host a gifted-and-talented consortium the following Wednesday with more than 40 people from over 20 districts, and said Project Lead the Way, an engineering and science curriculum, is planning a summit in the district in January.

Election season and what's next

Mr. Marozine noted that it is election season and asked members and candidates to keep things cordial. A resident said the board president and vice president are seeking re-election this year. The board's next regular meeting was set for October 14, 2026.

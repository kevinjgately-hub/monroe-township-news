Town Council · Full Meeting Recap

Everything that happened at Monroe's June 29 council meeting

Data centers, a landfill solar plan and about $6.8 million in borrowing all take their first step, residents press the council on data centers and a worn-out community center rink, and the engineer lays out a summer of road and rail-crossing work.

Jun 29, 2026 · Compiled from the official meeting recording, published on the township's YouTube channel

Quick facts

What
Monroe Township Council, combined agenda & regular meeting
Where
Monroe Township, Middlesex County, New Jersey
When
Monday, June 29, 2026; the recording runs about 1 hour 12 minutes and the meeting adjourned at 7:48 p.m.
Who was there
Councilwoman Miriam Cohen, Councilman Charles Dipierro, Council Vice President Michael Markel, Council President Rupa Siegel, Mayor Stephen Dalina and Business Administrator Kevin McGowan. Councilman Terence Van Dzura was absent.

A quick guide to how Monroe passes laws. An ordinance is a permanent local law, and it has to be voted on twice. The first vote, called first reading, is essentially an introduction: the council agrees to put the text on the record and schedule a public hearing. The second vote, second reading, comes after that hearing and is the vote that actually adopts the law. Almost everything the council voted on for ordinances at this meeting was a first reading, and the second readings came at the August 3 meeting, which we covered in its own recap. Smaller one-time decisions are resolutions, and the council usually passes a long list of routine ones in a single vote called the consent agenda. The meeting opened with the national anthem, kicking off the township's week of events for America's 250th birthday.

1. Data centers: a clean-up of old zoning language, and residents who want more

The most debated item of the night was Ordinance 06-2026-013, which removes the phrase "data processing" and similar language from the zoning rules for four kinds of zones: planned office commercial, highway development, light industrial and office professional. A data center is a large building of computer servers, the kind that power cloud services and artificial intelligence, and it uses a great deal of electricity and water. Township officials said they are confident data centers are not permitted anywhere in Monroe today, and described the old wording as vague language dating to 1991 that someone might try to read as allowing them. Deleting it, they said, is a case of "better safe than sorry." The council passed the ordinance on first reading 4–0, with a public hearing scheduled for August.

Two residents pressed the point during public comment, and the answers they got are the most useful part of the discussion. The first supported the ordinance but urged the council to go further and adopt a moratorium of at least 12 months on any data center application, citing the environmental and electricity-cost concerns of data centers elsewhere in New Jersey and what the resident described as a lack of state rules, and handed the council a letter that local residents are sending to the governor. Later a second resident asked whether the township is taking a proactive step or is "data center friendly." An administration official answered that no moratorium is needed, because under New Jersey's Municipal Land Use Law a use that isn't specifically permitted is prohibited by default, so the planning board could not accept an application for one. Once the ordinance is adopted, the official said, there will be no zone where a data center is a permitted use, and the council would have to act in the future to change that. During the council's own discussion, members asked what this means for land owned by the state or federal government, and the answer was that federal property can ignore local zoning but state property is generally subject to it, that officials knew of no state or federal land in the affected zones, and that a private buyer of any such land would have to follow the township's zoning. On the subject of a long-established state facility in town, an official said no sale was expected soon, with a look-ahead of four to seven years before anything might change.

2. The landfill solar plan and about $6.8 million in borrowing are introduced

Ordinance 06-2026-014 recommends adoption of a redevelopment plan for the township's old municipal landfill, covering several lots in blocks 148 and 148.60. A redevelopment plan is the document that decides what may be built on a site, and officials described the idea as a community solar project, a large array whose electricity is sold to local subscribers at a discount. As explained at the meeting, residents of Inwood Estates, the neighborhood that surrounds the landfill, would get first priority to subscribe, then the rest of Monroe, then people beyond Middlesex County. A resident asked who benefits, and the business administrator answered. The ordinance passed first reading 4–0 and was adopted on August 3.

The council also introduced two bond ordinances, which let the township borrow for specific projects. Bonds are the long-term loans municipalities use for big purchases like vehicles, roads and water systems. Ordinance 06-2026-015 would appropriate about $2.39 million for general capital projects and authorize about $1.88 million in bonds or notes, and Ordinance 06-2026-016 would authorize about $4.37 million for water and sewer projects. Both passed on first reading, 4–0. A council member noted during the meeting that a bond for the Applegarth water tank was among those before the council that night. Under New Jersey law a bond ordinance needs four yes votes from a five-member council, which became the whole story on August 3: the water and sewer bond was adopted, but the general bond got only three yes votes and failed, and it returned later in the year (see our September 2 and October 5 recaps).

3. A resident group asks the township to fix the community center roller rink

A representative of Monroe Township Hockey, a volunteer group that began in 2017 to let middle school and high school players keep playing together in the spring, asked the council to restore the roller rink at the community center to a safe condition. The speaker said the group now offers free floor hockey clinics and open gym programs and has introduced more than 75 Monroe children to the sport. The speaker also asked the council to publicly recognize the Monroe High School varsity hockey teams for winning back-to-back Greater Middlesex Conference championships in the 2023–24 and 2024–25 seasons, and members of those teams who attended stood to be recognized. The speaker thanked Councilman Dipierro for recognizing the teams at an earlier meeting and for starting the conversation about the rink, and said that after a meeting with township staff the week before, the group came away believing the township intends to keep the pavilion and rink. The group was not asking for a full renovation, the speaker said, only a first step of making the rink safe.

Councilman Dipierro followed up at the end of the meeting by asking when the community center parking lot and rink project would go out to bid. The business administrator said the hockey rink is not part of the parking lot design, and that there are shorter-term repairs the township can make to the rink itself. The engineer said the parking lot is still in design, that bids would go out late in the year if this year at all, and that paving depends on the weather, but that he would know more in about a month. Dipierro said that safety is his main concern and asked that the rink be repaired sooner rather than later. The administrator confirmed that work on the rink is "in process."

Also from Monday's meeting

Four more ordinances introduced

All passed on first reading, 4–0. Ordinance 06-2026-010 amends the township's fee chapter, including water and sewer connection fees (see "Water and sewer connection fees" below). Ordinances 06-2026-011 and -012 accept permanent easements on lots 30 and 27.3 of block 6, which give the township the right to use part of a private property for a specific purpose. Ordinance 06-2026-017 conveys part of the township's Spotswood-Englishtown Road property (block 54, lot 5.04) to Middlesex County so the county can make culvert improvements to the road.

Road and rail crossing work this summer

The township engineer reported that the five new pickleball courts and about 70 new parking spaces at the senior center are finished and open, and that the concession stand at James Monroe Park has power and a septic system and is waiting for a temporary certificate of occupancy. Concrete work for the 2026 road program is done, and milling and paving on roughly 18 roads (including Lynx Drive) was to begin in about two weeks. A utility contractor is repairing traffic signals at Schoolhouse and one other road, and the township is waiting on an inspection report covering all signals. The state planned two rail-crossing closures: Hoffman Station Road fully closed around the clock from Monday, July 20 through Friday, July 24 for new rails and paving, and Schoolhouse Road closed overnight on Tuesday, July 28. There was no update on the Prospect Plains Road crossing, where the county has limited its first phase to a signal after problems getting easements from the railroad.

Water and sewer connection fees

A resident asked about the fee ordinance and what it does to water and sewer bills. The business administrator said the township collected about $137,000 in connection fees in the first half of 2026, that the township now expects a good deal less revenue from them than earlier projections (he gave a rough range of $600,000 to $700,000 for the year, though the captions are unclear), and that the fees follow a state formula and went up by about $200 each for water and sewer this year.

A new snow truck, and liquor license renewals

The council's consent agenda covered resolutions 124 through 135. Councilman Dipierro asked that two be pulled out for separate votes, and both then passed. One renewed 18 liquor licenses, and we could not identify the other from the recording. Another resolution, for roughly $217,000 to $218,000, pays to fit out a new snow truck with a brine unit, a stainless-steel salt spreader, an 11-foot plow and related equipment. The truck itself was bought with bonds at the end of last year and was due to arrive at the end of July. A resident asked about the age of the township's trucks and whether buying the equipment as a package would cost less, and the business administrator said the truck's manufacturer does not supply the equipment. Dipierro abstained on the monthly payment of claims, and the council approved the June 3 minutes.

Council and mayor's remarks

Councilman Dipierro thanked the county for restriping the Halfacre Road and Forsgate Drive intersection, asked for a report on traffic lights whose emergency-vehicle preemption devices are not working, noted that the Route 522 water tower is finished and a fence is up at Well 19, and said trucks are cutting through near Route 33 and Bentley Road and backing up Route 33, which he asked the planning board to discuss. Councilwoman Cohen offered heat-safety tips and previewed the July 4 week schedule. Council President Siegel announced a ribbon cutting for a new restaurant on Route 33, listed the township's parks, and reminded residents of National Night Out on August 4. The mayor described the week's events: a concert at the senior center that night, a drone show the next evening, a library scavenger hunt, a concert in the park, a laser show at the community center, and the July 4 fireworks. A VFW 5K run and one-mile walk was also announced.

Proclamations

The council recognized a teacher retiring after 32 years in the Monroe schools, proclaimed July Park and Recreation Month, and presented a plaque to an employee retiring after 35 years with the township's EMS.

Other public comment

A resident asked that the township's poll workers be trained in Monroe rather than Piscataway, and we did not catch a response. A resident of a short street said a township sidewalk, curb and driveway-apron project on their block has been plagued by delays, a lack of utility markouts, and sod that was promised and never laid, and asked for another meeting with the mayor; the five-minute limit ended the remarks, and the recording does not show a response. A third resident said that red-light running, tailgating, passing on double lines, phone use and speeding (including by teenagers on scooters) are getting worse as the town grows, praised the police, and suggested the township look at red-light cameras, a way to submit dash-cam video, and publicized enforcement days. The council did not respond on the record.

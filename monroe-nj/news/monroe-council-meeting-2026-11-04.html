Town Council · Full Meeting Recap

Everything that happened at Monroe's November 4 council meeting

The agenda never made it onto the township website, the Heritage Chase repaving comes in about $300,000 under budget, a well stays offline while its filter material is replaced, and officials say Monroe has about 20 Flock license-plate cameras.

Nov 4, 2026 · Compiled from the official meeting recording, published on the township's YouTube channel · 8 min read

Quick facts

What
Monroe Township Council, agenda meeting followed by the regular meeting
Where
Monroe Township, Middlesex County, New Jersey
When
Wednesday, November 4, 2026, starting at 6:30 p.m. The regular meeting opened at 6:43 p.m. and adjourned at 7:23 p.m.; the recording runs about 53 minutes
Who was there
All five council members — Councilwoman Miriam Cohen, Councilman Charles Dipierro, Councilman Terence Van Dzura, Council Vice President Michael Markel and Council President Rupa Siegel — plus Business Administrator Kevin McGowan, the township engineer, the township attorney and the head of the township's water and sewer utility (MTUD).

This was a short meeting the day after Election Day, with no ordinances up for a vote. Council approved the month's routine resolutions in a single 5–0 vote, and most of the time went to updates from officials and questions from residents. Here's everything that happened, biggest stories first.

1. The agenda wasn't on the township website

The first speaker of the night said she had checked the township website several times, including that afternoon, and could not find the November 4 agenda; the newest one posted was from October 5. She said the public had no chance to review it before the meeting, that the law calls for agendas to be available about 48 hours ahead, and that at least two other township commission meetings had been canceled without notice she could find. She praised the township clerk, who she said had "a heck of a week" with the election, and said someone else should be responsible for checking postings.

The clerk said the agenda had been posted on the bulletin boards in the municipal building and uploaded to the website, but the website had not updated, and asked residents to call the office when something seems missing. She added that the two cancellations — the Cultural Arts Commission on October 19 and an Affordable Housing Board meeting — were posted under the new "Legal Notices" tab on the township homepage. She said her office controls those legal notices but not the township's separate events calendar. Asked by a council member for guidance, the township attorney said recent changes to New Jersey's Open Public Meetings Act let towns post legal notices online in one designated spot on their website instead of sending them through newspapers, and that a notice there satisfies the law even if a calendar elsewhere isn't updated. He called the missing agenda "an unfortunate error," said copies were sent to people who had asked for them, and said anyone can always ask the clerk's office for a copy of a current or past agenda.

2. The Heritage Chase repaving comes in about $300,000 under the estimate

Among the routine resolutions was the contract award for milling and repaving streets in the Heritage Chase neighborhood, a project with a state Department of Transportation grant behind it; the township engineer said work will start after the winter. Councilman Dipierro noted that the bond ordinance adopted in October (see our October 5 recap) authorized more borrowing than the bids now require. Administrator McGowan said the engineer's estimate, with all the optional extras, was about $1 million, and the low bid came in at about $744,000 from a field of six or seven bidders. He said that because the township borrowed against the estimate before the job went out to bid, it will now take out about $300,000 less in debt than planned, and that the low bidder is a contractor the township has used before without many change orders.

3. Well 16 stays offline, and new PFAS treatment is coming to Wells 17 and 19

Councilman Dipierro asked the head of the MTUD to explain a letter the utility recently mailed to residents. The director said the utility was not satisfied with new treatment material (the "media" that filters the water) a manufacturer installed in Well 16: the company guaranteed a level of performance, the utility's continuous testing showed the results were inconsistent, and after the manufacturer acknowledged a mistake in how it first filled the media, performance improved but still not enough. So he took the well out of service, and the utility is putting in brand-new media. He stressed that there is "absolutely no violation" of Safe Drinking Water Act rules, that the township holds itself to a stricter standard for this media than the state requires, and that there is no impact on residents' water use. Later, answering a resident, he said the well is still out of service and that the utility is pursuing the problem with the manufacturer "through legal means and other things."

The same resident asked about PFAS, the long-lasting "forever chemicals." The director said the township's water is well within state guidelines, that one well had also been taken out of service (and residents were notified), and that the utility has awarded a construction contract for new PFAS treatment at Wells 17 and 19, designed to meet stricter federal limits that take effect in 2029. A pre-construction meeting was set for the second week of November.

The director also gave an update on the Route 522 water tower. Earlier this year officials described it as essentially finished, but he said engineering and soil studies found a problem under the tank floor: a specialty contractor will cut small openings in the floor, inject grout to stabilize the base, test the patches for leaks, recoat the floor and return the tank to service, which he hoped would happen within a month or two.

4. Questions about Flock license-plate cameras

During public comment on agenda items, a resident asked whether a $450,000 public safety grant on the agenda (resolution 188) would be used for Flock cameras, which read and record license plates. Officials said no. Administrator McGowan later explained that the money was part of a state legislative appropriation in this year's state budget and is guaranteed for 2027; the township applies through the state Department of Community Affairs because that agency manages and distributes it.

In the general public comment period, the same resident asked whether Monroe uses Flock cameras now. McGowan said yes: he believes there are about 20 automated license-plate readers, with the locations chosen by the police department. The township attorney said the locations would not be available through an Open Public Records Act request, because records the police keep for investigations generally aren't public, though he said the resident was welcome to file a request — with the police department, which handles requests for police records. Another resident later said she was disappointed to learn the township uses the cameras.

Also from Wednesday's meeting

The other resolutions residents asked about

Answering questions from a resident, Administrator McGowan said resolution 191 releases a $78,000 affordable-housing lien on a home on Morning Glory Drive because the property was sold and the full amount is being repaid; resolution 195 refunds $19,843 in 2026 property-tax overpayments to several residents who have newly qualified as disabled veterans; and resolution 198 hires Garden State Laboratories, the low bidder, for water and sewer lab testing, a company that has worked for the township before and charges by the type of test rather than one contract price. Council also approved the monthly bills and the minutes of the October 5 meeting, each 5–0.

The Applegarth–Prospect Plains intersection won't be done before winter

The township engineer said the county's project at Applegarth Road and Prospect Plains Road is still held up by utility companies — JCP&L took about six months to move poles, Verizon and Comcast have had to move lines, and a gas main in the intersection still has to be relocated, with no schedule from the utilities. He said it will not be finished before winter and he is working with the county engineer on making the intersection safe and stable until spring. Council Vice President Markel said he raised it again because many people in the area are concerned.

Other road and building updates

The engineer said one street remains in the 2026 paving program, to be done in the next two weeks, with guide rail going in sometime in December; that General Electric has been authorized to repair the Opticom devices (which let emergency vehicles change traffic signals) over the next couple of weeks; and that dead landscaping at the senior center was replaced. He also warned of a closure of Pineville Road from Forsgate Drive to Dogwood Drive, the entrance to Thompson Park, starting Friday evening, November 6, at 7 p.m.; the work is in Jamesburg, but the detour affects Monroe. In answers to Councilman Dipierro, officials said the new automatic ADA-friendly restroom doors at the senior center are targeted for the end of the year, that the township has replaced the goals and repaired boards, glass and cracks at the recreation center's hockey rink, and that the rink's parking lot is still in design. Dipierro also pointed to large potholes on Monroe Boulevard, Federal Road and Spotswood-Gravel Hill Road and reminded residents that the public works page on the township website has a way to report them.

The Meadow View affordable-housing site

Asked about the former Meadow View nursing home site, Administrator McGowan said the township is working with a nonprofit affordable-housing developer that is designing the site and studying its wetlands and flood-hazard areas, and that the developer is also looking at some alternative sites in town in case an environmental obstacle turns up. He said the township has a full 10-year period to deliver this municipally sponsored affordable housing and that he is confident the site will work out.

The Training School for Boys

Councilman Dipierro, who chairs the Training School for Boys Citizens Review Board, said the state has two replacement facilities under construction in central and southern New Jersey, with completion expected toward fall 2027, and still has to start a third in the north. He said the school has about 110 residents, that all three facilities are needed before the residents can be moved, and that he hopes they are complete by 2028, after which the state would move the residents out.

Stolen ANCHOR checks, and a bus request for veterans

Dipierro said several residents, in adult communities and elsewhere, told him their state ANCHOR property-tax relief checks were stolen from mailboxes and cashed by someone else; he said the state reissued payment to the people who called him, and he asked the township to post an alert. He also relayed a request from the Knights of Columbus for two township buses to take veterans to a holiday luncheon on December 6 at 10:30 a.m., as the township has done in the past.

The best-practices checklist

Administrator McGowan reported that the township submitted the state's annual "best practices" questionnaire by the October 30 deadline; the state uses it to check towns' compliance with local finance, contracting and borrowing laws, and he said Monroe passed "with flying colors."

The election, and council members' remarks

Several council members thanked the clerk's office and poll workers for running the election, which Markel said kept staff at work from 5 a.m. to about 10:30 p.m. Councilman Van Dzura said it was inspiring to see so many people vote, criticized the tone of many campaign ads, and said he hoped people would come together now that voters have spoken. Councilwoman Cohen said the township's advisory health board had a table at Oktoberfest for the first time, handing out the "File of Life" (an emergency medical information card) and warning about lead in household products. She also described a Kristallnacht survivor about to turn 103 who was honored at Congregation Monroe Jewish Center, and spoke about Veterans Day as the child of a veteran.

What residents raised in public comment

For the third meeting in a row, a resident asked the council to put a resolution declaring Monroe a sanctuary city on its agenda, this time asking for the December 2 meeting; he said he may provide proposed wording, and there was no council response on the record. Another resident asked the township for a clear written answer on whether a private lane where her family has lived for more than 20 years was ever formally accepted as a public road, and what it would take to make it one; she said access to the lane is the subject of ongoing litigation. (The lane's name is garbled in the captions.) The township attorney cautioned that when a matter is in litigation, information is usually gathered through that court process rather than through a records request.

Coming up in Monroe

Council President Siegel said the October 27 small-business workshop, run with Middlesex County's Office of Business Engagement, was the first held in Monroe and drew a good turnout, and that the township's fifth annual Diwali celebration, themed "East Meets West," collected $850 from vendors for the local food pantry. Markel invited residents to the Veterans Day ceremony at 9:30 a.m. on November 11 at Veterans Park, and to the township's Christmas tree and menorah lightings. The next council meeting is Wednesday, December 2, at 6:30 p.m.
